The pain in my QA flow

Without a shared checklist, quality depended on who did the work, not on the process. Refinement skipped rules "everyone knew", sprint testing varied with the day's load, and regression became one person's memory. When that person was away, the findings were away too.

5 different routinesper sprint, no single source of truth

How a QA uses it

Each checklist answers one concrete question of the flow. I pick the phase I am in, and the checklist tells me what to verify before moving on.

QA questionWhere it gets answered
Is this story ready to enter the sprint?Shift-Left Testing Checklist — 41 refinement items: testable ACs, business rules, data and risks before estimating.
Am I covering full sprint testing with no gaps?Sprint Testing Checklist — 84 items: planning, execution, evidence and reporting for every ticket in QA.
What document do I leave the team, and with what ROI?Test Documentation Checklist — 36 TMS items: derive from the ATS, prioritize by ROI, keep traceability.
What do I demand before signing off an automated test?Test Automation Checklist — 30 KATA items: atomic ATCs, fixtures, selectors, no duplication.
Can I call GO for release?Regression Testing Checklist — 30 CI/CD items: classify failures and decide with data, not courage.

In my working kit

The checklists are not decorative documentation: they are the quality gates of my flow. I apply them in this order, on every ticket.

  • Pre-sprint: shift-left checklist as the story's entry gate to the sprint.
  • In QA: sprint testing checklist as the execution and evidence guide per ticket.
  • Documentation: test documentation checklist to decide what to automate first by ROI.
  • Automation: test automation checklist as the Definition of Done for test code.
  • Release: regression checklist as the GO / CAUTION / NO-GO gate.

What it demonstrates

Process standardization

I turn person-dependent routines into a process any new QA can follow from day one.

Shift-left testing

I move ambiguity detection into the story: testable ACs before estimating, not bugs after development.

Risk-based thinking

Every phase prioritizes what breaks the release, not a generic "test everything" list.

Closing

Five checklists, 221 items, one common language for the whole QA cycle. The repo is public: open them, clone them, adapt them to your team.
